Host–microbiota co-metabolism is critical for regulating human physiology, maintaining homeostasis, and influencing disease susceptibility. However, systems-level frameworks for quantitatively decoding the complex crosstalk between the gut microbiota and host organs remain limited. To address this, we developed a Host–Microbiota Co-metabolism (HMC) modeling framework that integrates constraint-based Gut Microbial Community (GMC) model, derived from microbiome sequencing data, with a whole-body Human Metabolic Model (HMM). This framework enables microbiome informed personalized metabolic flux simulations to systematically characterize microbial contributions to host metabolism, trace cross-organ metabolic alterations underlying disease pathophysiology, and identify combinatorial microbe-metabolite biomarkers for improved disease diagnosis via machine-learning.  In summary, this study leverages constraint-based HMC modeling and machine learning to decode host–microbiota metabolic crosstalk and identify novel combinatorial biomarkers. It provides a scalable strategy for exploring gut–brain interactions in psychiatric disorders, overcoming the limitations of directly profiling metabolic alterations in brain tissue or other organs.

- wholebodymodel_FBA.m: Constructs an integrated whole-body metabolic model by incorporating the individual gut microbiome models into the host metabolic model. The resulting model is used for flux balance analysis (FBA) and metabolic flux simulation.
- compareWBM.m: Compares flux values for selected reactions across different models to quantify and highlight the contribution of the gut microbiota to specific metabolic processes.
- contribution.m: Quantifies the contributions of different components to individual metabolites, enabling the assessment of their relative contributions to metabolite production or consumption.
- generate_model_flux_simulation.m: Generates an individualized gut microbiome community (GMC) model for each sample and performs metabolic flux simulations to characterize sample-specific metabolic activity.
- ML.R Implements a random forest machine learning algorithm for data analysis and predictive modeling.
